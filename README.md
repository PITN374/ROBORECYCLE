1. INTRODUCTION

ROBORECYCLE.ai and ROBOPARTS.ai are interconnected industrial technology platforms designed to recover, evaluate, document, reuse, source, and commercially distribute industrial robotics and automation equipment.

Their purpose is to preserve the remaining functional and economic value of industrial equipment after the machinery in which it was originally installed has been retired, replaced, upgraded, or decommissioned.

Industrial robots and manufacturing systems contain valuable components that may continue to function, may be repairable, or may serve as replacement equipment for other industrial applications.

Rather than treating an entire retired machine as scrap, ROBORECYCLE.ai provides a structured process for identifying and recovering qualifying components, evaluating their actual condition, and preparing suitable equipment for renewed industrial use.

ROBOPARTS.ai provides the commercial marketplace through which qualifying equipment can be presented, sourced, and distributed.

The fundamental relationship is simple:

ROBORECYCLE.ai recovers and evaluates industrial technology.
ROBOPARTS.ai markets and distributes qualifying industrial technology.

Together, they establish an integrated industrial recovery and secondary supply operation.

3. HOW ROBORECYCLE.ai WORKS

The recovery operation follows a defined sequence.

Stage 1 — Source

Identify industrial equipment available through:

Robotics manufacturers.
Manufacturing facilities.
Industrial equipment owners.
Factory modernization projects.
Production-line decommissioning.
Retired robotic installations.
Industrial system integrators.
Equipment maintenance organizations.
Industrial auctioneers and recovery partners.
Stage 2 — Identify

Register and document available equipment.

Record manufacturer, model, part number, serial number where available, product revision, photographs, and original equipment association.

AI-assisted image recognition and optical character recognition can support identification.

Stage 3 — Acquire and Recover

Establish acquisition or recovery arrangements and arrange appropriate equipment removal.

Qualified personnel recover individual components and assemblies suitable for further evaluation.

Stage 4 — Inspect and Test

Evaluate recovered equipment through appropriate procedures, including:

Physical inspection.
Electrical testing.
Functional testing.
Manufacturer-specific diagnostics where available.
Damage and deterioration assessment.
Repairability evaluation.

Testing is appropriate to the equipment type and available technical documentation.

Stage 5 — Grade

Assign a condition classification supported by inspection and testing records.

Classifications include:

Inspected.
Tested Functional.
Untested.
Repair Required.
Refurbished.
Parts Recovery.
Material Recovery.
Recycling.

A visual inspection alone does not establish functional condition. Compatibility with another industrial system must be evaluated separately.

Stage 6 — Document

Create an individual digital equipment record containing available information about:

Manufacturer and model.
Part and serial numbers.
Original equipment association.
Acquisition source.
Physical condition.
Inspection results.
Electrical and functional testing.
Identified defects.
Repair and refurbishment history.
Condition classification.
Provenance and custody.
Commercial disposition.
Stage 7 — Qualify Inventory

Determine whether each item is suitable for resale, repair, refurbishment, further parts recovery, material recovery, or recycling.

Ownership and resale authorization must be established before commercial distribution.

Stage 8 — Distribute

Transfer qualifying commercial inventory into ROBOPARTS.ai for product cataloging, marketplace presentation, sourcing, and distribution.

Stage 9 — Recirculate

Make qualifying equipment available for appropriate industrial maintenance, repair, replacement, and production applications.

5. WHY ROBOTICS MANUFACTURERS SHOULD USE ROBORECYCLE.ai

ROBORECYCLE.ai gives robotics manufacturers a structured way to recover value from equipment that would otherwise be stored, discarded, sold without component-level evaluation, or sent for recycling.

A. Recover Residual Equipment Value

When robotic systems are replaced, their individual components may retain commercial value.

ROBORECYCLE.ai identifies potentially reusable equipment, evaluates its condition, and establishes appropriate recovery and disposition pathways.

Manufacturers gain an opportunity to recover value from individual components rather than treating an entire retired robotic system as undifferentiated scrap.

B. Establish Additional Replacement Inventory

Manufacturers frequently support older robotic systems that remain operational at customer facilities.

Some replacement components become discontinued, expensive, or difficult to source.

Recovered equipment can provide an additional potential source of replacement inventory for maintenance and service requirements.

C. Support Older Installed Robotic Systems

Manufacturers can use recovered components to support customers operating previous generations of robotic equipment.

Where technically suitable and properly authorized, recovered inventory can support:

Maintenance programs.
Repair operations.
Replacement requirements.
Service inventory.
Legacy equipment support.
D. Outsource Equipment Recovery

Manufacturers do not necessarily need to establish their own complete equipment recovery, testing, refurbishment, and secondary distribution operation.

ROBORECYCLE.ai can provide an organized recovery pathway through agreed service arrangements and qualified technical partners.

This allows manufacturers to focus on their primary manufacturing and customer-support operations.

E. Improve Equipment Lifecycle Visibility

Recovery projects generate useful information about industrial equipment.

Manufacturers can obtain insight into:

Which components are being retired.
Which products remain recoverable.
Which components require repair.
Which models are becoming obsolete.
Which replacement parts are difficult to obtain.
Which equipment retains commercial demand.
Which recovery activities produce positive economic results.

This information can support service planning, product lifecycle management, replacement strategies, and future equipment development.

F. Support Circular Manufacturing

Recovering and reusing qualifying equipment can reduce unnecessary disposal and extend the productive life of existing industrial technology.

Documented recovery, reuse, refurbishment, and material recycling activities provide measurable information to support manufacturer sustainability initiatives.

Recovered equipment is not represented as equivalent to new equipment.

G. Create Additional Commercial Opportunities

Manufacturers can participate through equipment sales, recovery agreements, surplus inventory programs, contracted recovery services, or other mutually agreed commercial arrangements.

The objective is to create an additional economic pathway for retired industrial technology.

6. HOW MANUFACTURERS CAN USE THE SERVICES

ROBORECYCLE.ai can support manufacturers through several practical service arrangements.

1. Factory Modernization Recovery

When a manufacturing facility replaces an existing production line, ROBORECYCLE.ai evaluates the removed equipment and identifies components suitable for recovery.

2. Robotic Fleet Replacement

When multiple industrial robots are retired, ROBORECYCLE.ai evaluates their controllers, drives, motors, sensors, electronic boards, and accessories for potential recovery.

3. Manufacturer Surplus Inventory

Manufacturers can submit excess, discontinued, returned, or surplus equipment for identification, evaluation, and potential distribution.

4. Returned Equipment Evaluation

Returned equipment can be inspected to determine whether it is suitable for resale, repair, refurbishment, parts recovery, or recycling.

5. Obsolete Component Recovery

Manufacturers can identify discontinued components that may remain useful to customers operating older robotic systems.

6. Recurring Recovery Agreements

Manufacturers can establish ongoing arrangements covering equipment removed from service through regular modernization, replacement, and retirement programs.

7. Decommissioning and Asset Recovery

Manufacturers can arrange recovery services for larger industrial projects involving complete robotic installations, production systems, or manufacturing facilities.
